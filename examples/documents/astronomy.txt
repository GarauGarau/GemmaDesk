Observing the night sky

A small telescope can reveal craters on the Moon and the bright moons of Jupiter. Choose a dark location away from street lamps and let your eyes adjust before observing. A stable tripod matters more than extreme magnification.

Stars appear to move across the sky because Earth rotates. An equatorial mount can follow this motion during a long exposure. Astronomers combine many photographs to reduce noise and reveal faint structures in distant galaxies.

Keep an observing notebook with the date, weather and objects you identify. Comparing observations over several months makes changes in planetary positions easier to understand. Never point a telescope at the Sun without suitable solar observing equipment.
